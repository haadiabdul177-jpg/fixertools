How BMI is calculated

BMI equals your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres, squared. If you use feet, inches and pounds, the calculator converts them first, so you get the same result either way.

For adults, a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is usually considered the healthy range. The healthy weight shown for your height is the weight that gives a BMI between those two numbers.

BMI works best as a quick starting point. It can be misleading for athletes, older adults and pregnant women, and it is not meant for children and teenagers, who use growth charts instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is BMI?

BMI (body mass index) is a number that compares your weight to your height. It is a quick screening measure, not a diagnosis.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI equals your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. With pounds and inches, the calculator converts them to kilograms and metres first.

What is a healthy BMI for adults?

For adults, a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is usually considered the healthy range. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight and 30 or more is obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

No. BMI does not measure body fat or muscle, so it can be misleading for athletes, older adults and pregnant women. It is also not meant for children and teenagers, who use growth charts instead.

Are my numbers saved or uploaded?
